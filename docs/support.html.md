# Baby Docs: Support

> Help and contact for the Baby Docs newborn paperwork app by Jack Wallner.

Web page: https://jackwallner.com/ios/babydocs/support.html

## Baby Docs Support

Last updated: August 23, 2026

## Getting started

On first launch the app asks seven screens of questions: your baby's date
of birth and the state the birth was registered in, the state you live in
and the parents' situation, how the family is covered, and then one screen
each for parental leave, the newborn account, a 529 and a passport. Every
one of those answers changes what ends up in the plan, which is why it
does not ask for anything else. Where an answer is not known yet, "not
sure" and "prefer not to say" are real choices: the plan says what is
pending rather than guessing.

When you finish, the Plan tab shows the tasks that apply to you, soonest
deadline first.

## Why a task is on my list

Tap it. The first thing on the detail screen is one sentence about why it
applies to *your* household, and at the bottom is a link to the
government page the rule came from, with the date that page was last read.
If a task does not apply to you, tap **Does not apply to us**
and it stays off the list even when the plan is rebuilt.

## The two dates that actually close

A job-based health plan must offer at least 30 days after a birth to add
the baby. Marketplace coverage is generally 60. Those are the only two the
app will send you a notification about, deliberately: an app that pings
you about everything is an app you turn notifications off for.

Your own plan may allow longer. It may not allow less. Check the plan
documents if the date is close.

## My state's birth certificate office

Birth certificates are issued by state or local offices, and the rules
differ everywhere. Where we have read a state's own page and can say
something specific, the task links straight to that office and Settings
lists the state as verified. Everywhere else it links to the federal
directory, which has a state-by-state picker. We would rather send you
somewhere general and correct than somewhere specific and guessed.

## Sending the plan to the other parent

On the Plan tab, tap the three-dot button and choose
*Send this plan to the other parent*. They tap the link and their
phone builds the same plan: the same tasks, the same dates, and reminders
of their own. It is free, and neither of you needs an account, because
there are no accounts.

The link carries your household answers and nothing else. It does not
carry what either of you has done, so the two plans run independently from
that point, so ticking something off on one phone does not tick it on
the other. That is a real limitation and it is worth knowing about rather
than discovering.

If the link does not open, some message apps cut long links in half. Send
it again, or answer the questions directly: it takes about a minute.

## Keeping copies of documents

The Documents tab holds photographs of the birth certificate, the Social
Security card, the insurance card and anything else you add, so you have
them at a counter without going home. They are stored on that iPhone only,
unreadable while the phone is locked, and deliberately excluded from every
iCloud and computer backup.

That last part has a consequence worth stating plainly: a new iPhone will
not restore them. Keep the original documents where you always kept them.
What is in the app is a convenience copy, never the record.

## Reminders are not arriving

Check iPhone Settings > Notifications > Baby Docs, and check
Settings inside the app. The two hard-deadline warnings are free. Plus can
also schedule suggested dates, a Sunday digest and reminders you set
yourself. All are scheduled on your own phone and need notification
permission, but not a Baby Docs server.

## Something I sent has not come back

Follow-up tracking is part of Baby Docs Plus. On a task you have posted
off, turn on *Sent it* and enter the date the office told you to
expect it. When that date passes, the task moves to the top of the plan
and says so. No turnaround time is guessed on your behalf: processing
times differ by county and change constantly, so the only figure worth
acting on is the one you were actually given.

## Purchases and refunds

Settings > Restore purchases brings back a purchase on a new phone or
after a reinstall. Purchases and subscriptions are handled by Apple, so
refunds are requested through Apple, not through us.

## Deleting your data

Archive a child or dismiss a task to remove it from the active plan. Its
record stays on this phone so you can restore it without losing work.
Removing a document from the vault deletes the photograph from the phone
immediately. Deleting the app removes the household data stored on that
phone; purchase records held by Apple and RevenueCat are separate.
