Baby Docs Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 15, 2026

Overview

Baby Docs is an iPhone app for keeping track of the paperwork that follows a birth: the Social Security card, the birth certificate, health plan enrollment, parental leave and so on. It tells you what applies to your household, when each window closes, and which official office issues each thing.

Baby Docs has no account and no household-data backend. There is nothing to sign in to, and we hold no copy of your child, your household answers, your plan, your notes or your documents. Almost everything below is a description of what stays on your own iPhone.

There is one exception, and it is worth stating at the top rather than burying it: if you buy Baby Docs Plus, purchase information goes to Apple and to RevenueCat, the service that tells the app whether a purchase is still valid. That is the only thing about you that ever reaches a server of ours, it contains nothing about your family, and it is described in full under Purchases and Baby Docs Plus.

The one thing we do not collect

Baby Docs never asks for, stores or transmits a Social Security number, for your child or for you. The app tracks whether the number has been requested and whether the card has arrived. It has no field for the number itself, and neither the one-page summary nor the employer packet ever prints one. If you type a number into a free-text note, it is saved as ordinary text on your phone like any other note, so please do not.

One clarification, because it matters. If you photograph a Social Security card into the document vault, that photograph obviously contains the number. It is stored as an image file on your iPhone only, held to iOS's strictest file protection, excluded from every backup, and no share sheet, export or summary in the app can reach it. We never receive it, because there is nowhere for it to go.

What is stored on your iPhone

  • Your child's first name, date of birth, the state and county the birth was registered in, and whether the Social Security card and birth certificate have arrived.
  • Your household answers: the state you live in, how the family is covered, whether the parents are married, and which optional things (passport, 529, parental leave) you want on the list.
  • The generated plan: each task, its deadline, which documents you have gathered, who it is assigned to, and any confirmation or tracking numbers you choose to record.
  • Any notes you write.
  • Photographs you choose to add to the document vault, and nothing about them beyond what you named them.

The local database is encrypted at rest by iOS until the first unlock after the phone boots. Vault photographs are held to a stricter setting again: unreadable whenever the phone is locked, and marked so they are never included in an iCloud or computer backup. That is a real trade-off and it is worth stating plainly — a new iPhone will not restore them, so keep the original documents where you always kept them.

What leaves your iPhone, and when

Only what you deliberately send, and only to whoever you send it to. There is no background upload, no account and no server copy.

  • Sending the plan to the other parent. This creates a link containing your household answers — the birth date, the state and county, how you are covered — so their phone can rebuild the same plan. The answers travel in the part of a web address that browsers never send to a server, so even though the link opens a page on our site, that page receives nothing. It carries no completed tasks, no confirmation numbers and no photographs.
  • The one-page summary and the employer packet. Plain text you generate and share yourself, to whoever you choose. Neither contains a Social Security number or a vault photograph.
  • Your purchase status, handled by Apple and RevenueCat. We never see a card number or any payment detail. The next section says exactly what those two receive.

Purchases and Baby Docs Plus

Everything the plan itself is made of is free: every task, every deadline, every document list, every official link, every reminder, and sending the plan to the other parent. None of it involves a network. If you never buy Plus, nothing in this section happens at all.

If you do buy Plus, the purchase is made with Apple, and Apple's own privacy policy covers it. To know whether that purchase is still valid, the app uses RevenueCat, which acts as our processor. What RevenueCat receives is:

  • An anonymous app user ID generated by the SDK. It is a random identifier for a copy of the app on a device. We do not link it to your name, your email, your Apple Account or anything you entered in the app, because the app never sends any of that.
  • Purchase history: which product was bought, when it started, whether a trial was used, whether it renewed, lapsed or was refunded, and the Apple receipt that proves it.
  • Basic technical context sent with those calls: app version, iOS version, device model, and the country of your storefront.

In Apple's App Privacy vocabulary that is Purchases → Purchase History, collected, not linked to your identity, not used for tracking, and used for two purposes: app functionality, which is unlocking what you paid for, and analytics, which is a dashboard of how many purchases, trials and refunds there were. It is never sold, never shared with advertisers, and never joined to data from other apps or websites. The app does not collect an advertising identifier and does not ask RevenueCat to collect a device one.

What RevenueCat never receives: your child's name or birth date, your state or county, your household answers, your plan, your notes, your confirmation numbers, and every photograph in the document vault. Those are not sent anywhere, by anything, ever.

Keeping it and deleting it. The purchase record is kept for as long as the purchase can still matter. A lifetime purchase has to be restorable on a new phone years later, which is the whole point of "restore purchases", and the record is also part of the transaction history Apple and we are required to keep. Deleting the app does not delete it, and cannot: a record that vanished with the app is a record that could not restore your purchase. If you want the RevenueCat record for your copy of the app deleted, email the address at the bottom of this page and we will delete it; be aware that doing so may mean a purchase can no longer be restored automatically, and that Apple keeps its own record either way.

Deadline reminders are scheduled by your own iPhone from dates it already holds. There is no push notification service involved and no device token, which is why reminders keep working with no signal at all.

If you tap “Use my location” on the household questions, iOS gives the app a single position, which is turned into a state and county on the device and then discarded. No coordinate is saved or sent.

Who can see it

Your child, your household answers, your plan, your notes and your documents: you, and anyone holding your iPhone. That is the whole list. We have no database of any of it, so there is nothing for us to look at, nothing to hand over and nothing to breach.

Your purchase record: Apple, RevenueCat and us, as described above. It says that a copy of the app bought a subscription. It does not say whose, and nothing in it is about a child.

This is why the plan opens with no network: the moment you most need to know which three documents the clerk is about to ask for is while you are standing at the counter.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, no ad identifiers, no third-party trackers.
  • We do not sell your family or app data or share it with advertisers. Purchase records are shared with Apple and RevenueCat as described above.
  • We do not file, submit or transmit any government, tax, insurance or legal form on your behalf. The app links you to the official page and you file it yourself.
  • We do not read, hold or connect to your health records, your insurance account, your bank or any government account.

Children

Baby Docs is for parents, and is not directed at children. The information it holds about a child is the small set listed above, entered by that child's own parent.

Deleting things

  • Delete a child, a task or a note in the app and it is gone from your plan.
  • Removing a document from the vault deletes the photograph from the phone immediately.
  • Deleting the app removes everything it holds about your family. There is no account to close and no server copy of any of it to chase, because neither exists.
  • The one thing deleting the app leaves behind is the purchase record held by Apple and RevenueCat, which is what lets a reinstall restore what you paid for. See Purchases and Baby Docs Plus for how to have ours deleted.

Your rights

You can see, correct, export (the one-page summary shares as plain text) and delete everything the app holds, from inside the app, without asking us. If you would rather we did it, email the address below.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who can see it, the app will say so before the change takes effect.
